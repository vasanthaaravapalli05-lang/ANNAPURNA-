## 2. What SDG 2 means

SDG 2 is the UN goal to **end hunger, improve food security and nutrition, and promote sustainable agriculture**. It asks for more than having food around. People need steady access to enough safe, nutritious food, and the systems that grow it have to last.

These targets matter most for food-related projects:

- **Target 2.1 – Access to food:** Everyone, especially people in vulnerable situations, can get safe, nutritious and sufficient food all year.
- **Target 2.2 – Better nutrition:** All forms of malnutrition are addressed, taking different groups' nutritional needs into account.
- **Target 2.3 – Small-scale food producers:** Small producers become more productive and earn more, partly through better access to resources and markets.
- **Target 2.4 – Sustainable agriculture:** Food production becomes more sustainable and better able to cope with climate and environmental pressure.

The targets give the team a way to test whether the project really is relevant to SDG 2. Having "food" in the name or focus isn't enough. What the project actually does, and what that produces, has to match a target.

The official descriptions come from the UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs and the UN Statistics Division. Links are in the references.

## 3. Where food-waste measurement fits

Measuring food waste connects more directly to **SDG 12.3**, the target on reducing food loss and waste. Its indicator has two parts:

- **12.3.1(a) – Food Loss Index:** The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) tracks losses along the supply chain, from after harvest up to (but not including) retail.
- **12.3.1(b) – Food Waste Index:** The UN Environment Programme (UNEP) measures waste at retail and consumer level, including food service and households.

The split matters because it shapes what a project measures and how it reports. Food lost after harvest is treated differently from food thrown out by a restaurant or a household.

If recording or measuring food waste is ANNAPURNA's main job, SDG 12.3 is the clearest fit. Those measurements might also guide SDG 2 work. But a lower waste figure doesn't automatically mean people have better access to food or better nutrition. The project would have to show how its activities lead there.

## 4. Possible ways ANNAPURNA could support SDG 2

The examples below show how different food-related activities might support SDG 2. They're here to help the team judge the project's direction. None of them is a confirmed ANNAPURNA activity.

| Possible activity (confirm with the project team) | How it could relate to SDG 2 | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Stopping edible food from being lost before it reaches consumers | Could help keep food available and make food systems more resilient (Targets 2.1 and 2.4) | The supply-chain stage, how much edible food was saved, the measurement method and a reliable baseline |
| Collecting and safely redistributing surplus edible food | Could help people who struggle to get enough food (Target 2.1) | Records of food delivered and accepted, food-safety checks and evidence about the intended recipients |
| Improving the nutritional quality of the food provided | Could support better nutrition (Target 2.2) | Food or nutrition standards, suitable measures of dietary quality and review by someone with the right expertise |
| Helping small-scale producers reach markets or earn more | Could support small-scale producers (Target 2.3) | Records of which producers took part and evidence of changes in income, productivity or market access |
| Supporting more sustainable, resilient production methods | Could support sustainable agriculture (Target 2.4) | Details of the practices introduced and records showing what changed |
| Measuring waste at shops, food-service sites or households | Directly supports SDG 12.3 and may inform SDG 2 work | A clear measurement boundary, a reliable baseline and an explanation of how the findings will be used |

Once the activities are settled, keep only the connections that really apply. And describe a benefit as a result only if there's evidence it happened.

## 6. What could the team measure?

The right measures depend on the final scope. These are options for discussion, not targets ANNAPURNA has agreed to.

| Area | Example measure | What it can tell us |
|---|---|---|
| Food-waste prevention | Waste measured before and after an activity, using the same method and scope | Whether the measured amount changed. On its own, it doesn't show that food access improved. |
| Surplus food recovery | Weight of safe edible food collected and accepted by a receiving organisation | How much food was recovered or passed on, though not necessarily whether anyone ate it or got better nutrition from it. |
| Food access | Number of intended households or people receiving food, how often, and how much | Helps describe access when delivery and receipt are recorded. It doesn't prove long-term food security. |
| Nutrition | Food types, nutritional composition or a suitable measure of diet quality | Can help assess nutritional value if the method is suitable. Weight alone can't show nutritional benefit. |
| Producer support | Number of producers involved, plus income, productivity or market access | Can show changes for producers, given reliable records and a fair comparison. |
| Sustainable production | Measures of resource use, efficiency or resilience | Should be chosen to fit the specific agricultural activity. |

It's too early to set numerical targets. First the team needs a reliable starting point and a clear sense of which outcomes the project can realistically influence.
