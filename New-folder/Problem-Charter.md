## 1. Purpose of the charter

Before a team builds anything, everyone should share the same picture of the problem: what's happening, who it affects, where it happens and why it matters. A problem charter pulls those points into one place. It also gives the team a basis for deciding what's in scope and how to judge progress.

The ANNAPURNA tracker asks for two pieces of background work: an explanation of the project's possible link to SDG 2, and research on food-waste measurement. What it doesn't tell us is the actual problem ANNAPURNA is meant to solve, what the proposed solution will do, who will use it or where it will run. There are no project results either.

That makes this a **working draft**, not an approved description of the project. It suggests a possible direction based on the tracker's wording, but the project owner needs to confirm it, or swap in the correct problem, before the charter is signed off.

## 3. A possible problem statement to discuss

Going by the food-waste measurement task, one starting point could be:

> The ANNAPURNA team may need a consistent way to understand and record food-related loss or waste in a clearly defined setting. Without reliable baseline information and agreed measurement methods, it may be hard to see where avoidable waste occurs, compare results over time or judge whether an improvement has worked.

This is a **working hypothesis** and nothing more. Until the project owner checks it, don't treat it as the project's confirmed problem. ANNAPURNA may have a different focus, and if so the statement should change.

### Other directions to consider

The project owner should decide which of these, if any, matches the project:

1. **Measuring food waste:** The relevant locations don't keep consistent records of how much food is thrown away, what kinds, or where it goes.
2. **Preventing food waste:** Food is wasted during a particular activity or stage, and the project aims to cut that waste.
3. **Recovering surplus food:** Safe, edible surplus is available while some people struggle to get enough to eat, and the project may aim to connect the two.
4. **Addressing a supply-chain issue:** A problem in production, storage, transport or another stage is hurting food availability or causing avoidable loss.
5. **Something else:** ANNAPURNA may have a purpose the tracker doesn't describe.

Each of these is a different challenge, with different users, activities, data and measures of success. The team should pick the one that fits the project, not blend several into one broad statement without a good reason.

## 4. Problem statement to complete with the project owner

Once the project owner confirms the details, the final problem statement can follow this structure:

> **[Target group]** in **[specific location or setting]** faces **[clearly described problem]** during **[activity or stage of the process]**. This matters because **[documented effect on food access, nutrition, producer income, operating costs, resource use or another relevant outcome]**. The available evidence shows **[verified observation or baseline]**, collected using **[method or source]** during **[time period]**. Current approaches don't fully address the problem because **[evidence-backed gap]**. ANNAPURNA plans to address or investigate this through **[confirmed project activity]**. Progress will be checked using **[agreed measures]**.

Don't fill the blanks with guessed figures, invented user experiences or unverified claims. If the evidence isn't there yet, say so and list what still has to be collected.

## 5. How the project may connect to the SDGs

### 5.1 SDG 2 – Zero Hunger

SDG 2 is about ending hunger, improving food security and nutrition, and promoting sustainable agriculture. Depending on what ANNAPURNA actually does, the most relevant targets may be:

- **Target 2.1:** Better access to safe, nutritious and sufficient food.
- **Target 2.2:** Tackling malnutrition and improving nutrition.
- **Target 2.3:** Supporting the productivity and incomes of small-scale food producers.
- **Target 2.4:** Encouraging sustainable food production and resilient agricultural practices.

The team should name the target that genuinely matches the planned activity and explain why. Measuring food waste doesn't show that hunger has fallen. Any claim about food security or nutrition needs a clear link to the project's work and evidence of the outcome.

### 5.2 SDG 12.3 – Food loss and waste

If ANNAPURNA measures food loss or waste, or works to reduce it, SDG 12.3 applies directly. Its indicator separates food loss earlier in the supply chain (12.3.1(a), measured by FAO) from food waste at retail and consumer level (12.3.1(b), measured by UNEP).

The first step is to work out where in the supply chain the issue occurs. That choice decides which definition, measurement approach and indicator the project should use.

### 5.3 Details still to establish

The tracker doesn't give us the project location, sector, target group, current process, food categories, scale, proposed activity or existing alternatives. We need all of these before the project context and SDG connection can be finalised.

## 8. How success could be measured

Pick measures only after the team agrees on the problem and what the project will actually do. The examples below can guide that discussion, but they aren't approved ANNAPURNA targets.

| Area | Possible measure | What it would tell us |
|---|---|---|
| Quality of baseline data | Percentage of records with a date, site, unit, method and destination filled in | Whether the data is complete enough to use. It says nothing about social impact. |
| Amount of waste measured | Total food-waste weight by site, category, material type and destination | The amount and type of waste recorded, as long as the measurement boundary is clear. |
| Waste compared with activity | Waste per meal, per kilogram of food input, per household or another suitable measure | Helps compare periods or sites when the same denominator is used consistently. |
| Waste prevention | Change in the food-waste rate after an intervention | Helps show whether waste changed, provided the baseline and follow-up methods are comparable. |
| Surplus food recovery | Amount of safe edible surplus accepted by a receiving organisation | How much food was recovered, but not whether it was eaten or improved nutrition. |
| Food access | Verified delivery and receipt of food by the intended recipients | Whether food reached the people it was meant to reach. |
| Nutrition | A suitable measure of food quality or diet quality, if nutrition is part of the project | Needs a method appropriate to the intended group, reviewed by someone with nutrition expertise. |
| Producer support | Changes in producer income, productivity or market access, if this is part of the project | Needs reliable producer-level information and a suitable comparison. |

Set numerical targets only once there's a baseline and the team understands which changes the project can reasonably influence.

## 9. Assumptions and risks

### Assumptions that need checking

- ANNAPURNA has a clearly documented food-related objective.
- Food-waste measurement is part of the project, or is needed to support its work.
- The team can get to the sites or records it needs to understand the problem.
- A useful baseline can be collected.
- The people involved can agree on common definitions, boundaries and units.

These are things to check with the project owner, not facts to take for granted.

### Main risks and how to reduce them

| Risk | Why it matters | How to reduce it |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing a solution before understanding the problem | The team may build something that doesn't meet the real need. | Talk to the relevant stakeholders and record the evidence before finalising the problem statement. |
| Mixing up food loss and food waste | The team may use the wrong definitions or make unfair comparisons. | Identify the supply-chain stage and follow the relevant FAO or UNEP guidance. |
| Changing measurement methods between periods | A difference in results may come from the method, not a real change. | Keep the method consistent and record any changes. |
| Treating estimates as measured quantities | The project may report more certainty than the data supports. | Label estimates clearly and explain how they were calculated. |
| Mixing food, inedible parts and packaging | The reported food-waste total may be misleading. | Agree on categories and separate material types where possible. |
| Treating food redistribution as proof of better food security | The amount delivered doesn't prove recipients ate it or benefited nutritionally. | Record safe delivery and receipt, and use suitable follow-up measures for any wider outcome claim. |
| Collecting unnecessary personal information | People or organisations may be exposed to privacy risks. | Collect only what's needed, limit access and follow appropriate privacy safeguards. |
| Missing project references | The team can't trace claims back to the original project information. | Request REF-ANN-01, REF-ANN-02 and REF-ANN-07, or record that they're unavailable. |

## 11. Questions for the project owner

The project owner should work through these before approving the charter:

1. ANNAPURNA is the project name. What does the project do? Please provide an approved project summary.
2. What problem led to the project, and what evidence shows it exists?
3. Who is affected, where does it happen and how often?
4. What does the project plan to do about it? What won't it try to solve?
5. Is food-waste measurement the main purpose, a supporting activity or just part of the background research?
6. Which food-system stage and sector are included?
7. Which SDG 2 target, if any, connects directly to the planned activity? Is SDG 12.3 relevant too?
8. Are there any pilot records, interviews, observations, operating logs or baseline measurements?
9. What do people do about the problem now, and what evidence shows where those approaches fall short?
10. Who will approve the problem statement and check that the evidence is reliable?
11. Where can the team find the original sources listed as REF-ANN-01, REF-ANN-02 and REF-ANN-07?

## 12. When can this charter be approved?

The charter can move to approval once the team has confirmed that:

- The project owner has confirmed the problem and the planned activity.
- The users, beneficiaries and operating context are clearly identified.
- The location and the process or food-system stage are defined.
- The problem statement rests on evidence, not just assumptions.
- Any link to SDG 2 or SDG 12.3 is explained accurately and isn't overstated.
- The team has agreed on suitable measures and understands the difference between outputs and outcomes.
- The assumptions, risks, exclusions and unanswered questions are visible.
- The missing internal references have been provided, or their absence has been recorded.
- The reviewer and approval details have been entered in the task tracker.
